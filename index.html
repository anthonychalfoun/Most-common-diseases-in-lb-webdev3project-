Most Common Diseases in Lebanon

Understanding the most prevalent health conditions and their impact on Lebanese society.

Lebanon has a significant burden of non-communicable diseases (chronic diseases), particularly cardiovascular diseases, cancer, diabetes, and respiratory diseases. Infectious diseases also remain important, especially seasonal respiratory infections and diseases linked to sanitation and vaccination coverage.

Chronic diseases

Long-term conditions that develop over years and need ongoing care.

Cardiovascular diseases

Heart disease and stroke are among the leading causes of death in Lebanon. High blood pressure, high cholesterol, and smoking are the main drivers.

Diabetes

Type 2 diabetes is increasingly common, linked to diet, weight, and low physical activity. Many cases are only found after complications appear.

Respiratory diseases

Asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) are made worse by tobacco smoke, including waterpipe, and by air pollution.

Infectious diseases

Illnesses spread by viruses, bacteria, or parasites.

Respiratory infections

Influenza, COVID-19, and other seasonal infections peak in winter and affect children and older adults the most.

Water and food-borne illnesses

Hepatitis A, typhoid, and cholera spread when water or sanitation systems fail. Lebanon saw a cholera outbreak in 2022.

Vaccine-preventable diseases

Measles outbreaks return when vaccination coverage drops. Routine childhood vaccination is the best protection.

Cancers

Cancer is one of the leading causes of death in Lebanon, and early detection saves lives.

Breast cancer

The most common cancer among Lebanese women. Regular screening and mammograms help find it early.

Lung cancer

Closely tied to cigarette and waterpipe smoking. It is one of the most common cancers in men.

Colorectal cancer

Affects both men and women. Screening from middle age can catch it before symptoms start.

Risk factors

Many of these can be reduced through prevention and access to care.

Lifestyle

  • Tobacco use, including waterpipe
  • Diet high in salt, sugar, and fat
  • Low physical activity

Environment

  • Air pollution
  • Unsafe water
  • Waste and sanitation problems

Health system

  • High cost of care and medication
  • Medicine shortages
  • Limited screening and follow-up

Hotline

Ministry of Public Health hotline: 1214

The Ministry lists 1787 or 1214 for urgent health support. The ministry has said patients can use these numbers for cancer-related assistance.

Lebanese Red Cross ambulance: 140

This is the ambulance line for medical emergencies, and the Red Cross general line is 1760

Civil Defense: 125

This is the emergency line for urgent rescue and medical help. The Internal Security Forces are on 112
